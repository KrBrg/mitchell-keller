# VOICE

## Summary
Mitchell writes like a GTM technician who ships campaigns: short stacked lines, blunt operator slang (“cooked,” “galaxy-brain,” “lmfao”), and frameworks compressed into posts (worldview alignment, stealth offers, message-is-god). He teaches by flipping common advice (bigger dream outcome → less believable cold; signals serve arguments not targeting; cold email before LinkedIn). Spoken/YouTube register is longer, “and so,” walkthrough → principle, with concrete client stories (Unity studios, agencies under/$10M, Reddit stealth offer). Replies and giveaways are frequent; he giveaways workflows for replies (“Reply GMB”) and ranks tools loudly.

## Patterns
- Offer anatomy in plain language: specific person + why trust you; description vs offer contrast.
- Antitheses: technician/maintenance man; situations/markets; package/truck; curious/believe.
- Imperative stacks and “Don’t…” failure lists (how to fail GTM).
- Named frames: worldview alignment, stealth offer, secondhand leads, bet-on-you, intro offer.
- Numbers as segment proof (12.5% vs 0% interested) more than vanity brags in teaching posts.
- Tooling as leverage (Clay, Cloudflare, Claude Code) but subordinated to message/offer.
- Casual swearing and sarcasm with prospects who won’t send case studies / have “no offer.”
- Spoken: Hormozi value equation restated, then his add-on (worldview) with restaurant-language analogy.
- Live SOW→offer checklists and agent-ops hacks (secrets, worktrees, markdown automations) in short stacked lines.
- Decision-layer framing (Jev: not a chatbot; typed confidence; enter most loops) and industry-selection maxims (“growing industries”).
