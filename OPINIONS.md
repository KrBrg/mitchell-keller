## Offers (specific person / problem / method / believability)

- **Offer ≠ description:** “We build scrapers / we run cold email / we do LinkedIn content” are descriptions. An offer tells a *specific person* what you can do for them and why they should trust you — that is what gets a stranger to buy.
- **Believability beats dream-outcome inflation:** To a stranger, bigger promises often do the opposite; the bigger the dream outcome, the less believable it is. High-value buyers read “freer than free / pay you if it fails” race-to-the-bottom packaging as silly / can’t-do-it.
- **Worldview alignment:** Real lever isn’t only prior proof — it’s proving you understand them as well as they do (or better). It wraps the whole value equation: dream outcome gets more specific; speed/ease become believable because you’ve been in their world.
- **Insider language:** Speaking the market’s native tongue unlocks trust the way greeting someone in their parents’ language does — they stop auditing reviews/prices. Aim for insider status, not “businesses like yours.”
- **Mechanism + visible investments:** Find what the business is already investing in / pursuing and shove that mechanism into the dream outcome so the outcome feels possible *for them*.
- **If anyone could say it, it’s not interesting or trustworthy:** Generic claims die; specificity and situational proof create trust.
- **Cold-email offer has two jobs:** Make them curious. Make them believe you. Pain points, outcomes, case studies, percentages are ingredients — most founders pile ingredients without knowing the dish.
- **Flat offer fix:** Find what the market hates about every other player; position against that (e.g. “growth partner” vs “growth agency”). Job isn’t inventing a new offer — it’s naming the tired pattern and being the opposite.
- **Offer first, then signals:** Offer → then stack signals/data/relevance. You don’t always have to *build* the offer first — see what resonates, then build.

## Stealth offers & situations (not markets)

- **Stealth offer:** Must be uncovered; competitors wouldn’t think to make it; customers would love it. Don’t brainstorm/workshop it — extract it from the market.
- **Sources:** Closed-won/lost transcripts + what the *salesperson understood* (not transcript alone); unspoken blockers / hidden vetoes; Reddit and macro catalysts (regs, paradigm tech).
- **Situations > demographics / signal-chasing:** Target “VP of Sales who inherited a broken process 60 days ago,” not “VP of Sales at SaaS.” Hiring/funding signals are a race to the bottom when everyone hits the same triggers; use signals to craft *arguments*, not as the whole targeting thesis.
- **Technician vs maintenance man:** Diagnostic language that finds the underlying flow issue beats “I fix toilets / I do everything.”
- **Segment by situation → tailored offer → the one that pops is the stealth thread** that unravels the market.

## Reverse risk / guarantees / intro offers

- **During offer testing:** Keep tests to short interest statements; do **not** add guarantees or “do it free” gimmicks — they confound whether the offer itself is interesting.
- **Cold first-touch:** Vague guarantees suck / attract tire-kickers / wrong customers; “we’ll do everything for you” can *lower* perceived likelihood because the buyer knows variables you can’t control.
- **When you do guarantee:** Winning guarantees have **specificity** that makes them believable (falsifiable outcome + time), not “100% satisfaction.”
- **Bet-on-you / intro offers:** Require prospect effort/sacrifice first so the guarantee becomes the real offer and they’re betting on their own abilities — more believable than fully hands-off.
- **Free work needs a real qualification gate:** e.g. check fit before the free build; reject when not a fit so the gate is real (Unity/multiplayer example).
- **Good intro offer → reverse pitch inbound:** Strong enough intro offer lets you reverse-pitch people who cold-message you.

## Outbound agency GTM / cold email ops

- **Cold email first (for clients):** Messaging lab with low reputation risk vs LinkedIn; honest replies are data; graduate proven language to LinkedIn. LinkedIn complements — doesn’t replace.
- **The message is god:** Galaxy-brain workflows/signals don’t matter if the message sucks. Data tells WHO; message determines IF they respond. Workflow is the delivery truck; message is the package.
- **Copy trick — name the current state:** Reframe with a label they’ve never heard (“secondhand leads”) so they feel instantly seen.
- **Don’t surface the signal as “I noticed / I saw you engage with competitor”** in a way that becomes noise when everyone does it — use the insight without the race-to-the-bottom opener pattern.
- **IVC / asset-gated call (framework in corpus):** Value before the ask in some motions; diagnostic on opener vs asset.
- **Show-up & end-to-end:** Own stranger → booked motion; confirm/engage before meetings (LeadGrow site claims high show-up vs industry).
- **Fit filters (agency):** High enough LTV, big enough TAM, offer that already works, founder voice, team that ships, skin in the game — pass set-and-forget / unproven / committee theater.

## Market map / account+contact maxxing (incremental 2026-09-22)

- **Account-list maxxing before contact spend:** Build the widest defensible company map first (directories, registries, Maps, SEC, job boards, etc.), normalize by domain, cheap rules → qualify → segment → expand (Discolike only on clean seed) — only then pay for people. Company decisions are cheap; contact enrichment is not (~$6/10k homepage qualification cited in current workflow).
- **10x company pile before cutting:** If you need 1,000 usable companies, keep ≥10,000 in the working pile before cutting — room for exclusions, bad domains, regex/homepage failures, segments, requalification.
- **Allow uncertain; never force in:** Model labels in/out/uncertain with literal reasons; do not force uncertain into the market so the spreadsheet looks finished. Manually check ~50 decisions before scaling; change one rule per miss.
- **do_not_enrich_contacts until qualified+segmented:** Contacts stay locked until passing qualification, segment ID, stored reason, and either an observed signal or a written reason the parent segment needs none.
- **Weird titles expand the universe ~20%:** Full employee/title surfaces (e.g. Devolver Digital in gaming) catch roles normal searches miss — expanded available gaming-market contact universe by roughly 20%; rare titles can also reverse into new accounts.
- **Gaming outbound case:** Client with no outbound motion → ~$726k through outbound as main revenue driver and helped position the company to be acquired — started by maxing market data / weird titles / segmenting by real problems.
- **Contact waterfall:** Broad employee pull → score (starting model: 40 functional / 25 authority / 15 seniority / 10 location / 10 confidence) → enrich top 3–5 when metered → Serper/Parallel/gov/SEC/BBB name discovery when blank → email patterns only after name+domain → catch-all lower confidence → website regex last; record `no_contact_found` without dropping the company from the map.

## Outbound offer ranking (incremental 2026-09-23)

- **Rank offers by cold stranger→customer power:** Criteria for the 27-offer ranking — can it turn a stranger into a customer / get cold interest toward the offer — not “feels viral on LinkedIn.”
- **Loom teardown: S→B (time + founder/product-led):** Personalized video / offer-to-record can crush, but is time-consuming and needs product experience; drops from S to B for that reason. Don’t claim you’ve already recorded if you haven’t — offer to record if they’ll watch.
- **Gift card / charity as PS add-on, not the offer:** Frictionless “PS I’ll buy you pizza / gift card for the meeting” or charity-donation PS can be S-tier; charity above gift card (“karma maxing”). The gift/charity is not the offer itself.
- **Webinar invite = D for outbound:** “No one gives a flying fuck about your webinar” for cold outbound; niche exceptions only. Free workshop only rises if you already have a content base / viral LinkedIn.
- **Lookalike case study → A with volume proof:** Specific lookalikes still bang; stacking “seven firms just like yours” beats one logo flex.
- **Benchmark scorecard = S* for data-driven products:** Especially third-party or money-making benchmarks (unlimited-data test; EmailBison sequencer bench) — S with asterisk when the product is data-driven.
- **Free work upfront / permissionless value = S:** Free landing pages, UGC, ad creatives, cold copy/lists — AI makes giving real free stuff in email #1 easy; Jordan Crawford-style permissionless value.
- **Creative ideas pack (Eric classic) scales with your context:** Founder-led creative ideas stand out; programming unique business context into AI campaigns is the leverage.
- **Dinner/roundtable = A around events:** Scrape event pages → cold email → in-person dinner; cited 42 meetings at one event and deal flow.
- **Free audit / strategy-call+asset = D:** People don’t want “what’s wrong” or a pick-your-brain call — prefer opportunity report + action plan / creative ideas. Done-with-you setup and early-access waitlists are not cold-ready.
- **Guarantees still careful who they attract:** Broke SMBs via soft guarantees; larger businesses = risk reversal to test; “I’ll work forever until it’s done” is a stupid guarantee; money-back can sit ~B next to Loom.

## Judgment / communication / skills over AI (incremental 2026-10-05 — Unfiltered YT P4eFdWA_egM + WthZoHMDFow)

First-party solo videos on his second channel (Mitchell Keller Unfiltered).

- **AI won't replace GTM engineers — judgment is the moat:** Human value is “at an all-time high as a result of this leverage.” Agents have no desires or sense of finite time, learn from the average, and will fudge what “done” looks like; knowing which signal/workflow is worth pursuing is “the thing AI doesn't replace, judgment.” Agents lower the technical bar of entry; they don't pick what's worth building.
- **Communication is the #1 skill:** “the most important skill you can have is communication” — live with founders, clients, prospects; that's how you uncover how a market describes its pain in its own words, worth more than knowing how to build the workflow.
- **Build real skills, not “AI as the final frontier”:** “Skills are forged in the fire.” Treating AI as the final-frontier skill is backwards; AI will keep getting smarter but won't get real skills. Use AI to amplify judgment — deterministic pipelines (e.g. TriggerDev + Harvest API monitors with filtering/scoring, self-annealing agents reading goal/context md files) that you audit, with lots of expert human-in-the-loop.
- **AI-only taste reads as slop (hedonic treadmill):** The more people see AI text/images/lead magnets, the less novel they are; buyers' time is scarce and they detect sloppy work — AI is “really, really, really good at building things, but not knowing what to build.”
- **Redefine the playing field until experience doesn't matter:** Best companies/people “redefine criteria until experience does not matter” (xAI on compute/UX, Tesla casting, TikTok taking friends out of the feed). Agency version: pick a niche your own background wins (his example: nuclear-based supply chain logistics from his nuclear background); “your moat can be anything.”
- **Founder-brain business, not commoditize-and-hire:** His only downtrend came from following a mentor's “commoditize, hire, get myself out of the inner workings” advice — “Did not work for me.” His business runs on his brain; AI turns that into leverage.
